DeepSpeed · Ray-based pipeline parallelism (rdsp)

rdsp benchmark results

rdsp against Megatron-Bridge 0.6.0, TorchTitan and Megatron-MIMO, training Qwen3.5 vision-language models on H100 80GB. Runs from Oct 17 to Oct 19, 2026. Every number below was recomputed from its run log.

The answer
  1. On receipts (image-heavy rows, 128 × 2,048 per step), rdsp is fastest. 35B MoE on 2 nodes: 1.36× Megatron-Bridge, 1.43× TorchTitan. Dense 27B on 1 node: 1.28× and 1.18×.
  2. On NVIDIA's own recipe shape (MedPix, light images, 32 × 4,096), Megatron-Bridge is faster. 1.38× on the 35B, 1.09× on the 27B.
  3. On Megatron-MIMO's own layout, the two are within 7% (0.93× to 1.04×). MIMO does not run the 35B MoE.

Why rdsp wins on receipts

  • Receipts are image-heavy: 801 of each row's 1,016 tokens are image tokens (79%).
  • In a pipeline, the image encoder runs on the first stage. With 8 near-equal stages on the 35B, stage 0 takes 11.9 s per step and the others 3.4–5.4 s. Every stage waits for stage 0.
  • rdsp times its stages for 3 steps, then gives the encoder its own stage of 4 GPUs. All stages then take 3.7–4.9 s and the step drops from 9.60 s to 6.38 s (1.50×).
  • Megatron-Bridge and TorchTitan give every pipeline stage the same number of GPUs, so they cannot build that layout. Bridge's best instead uses 2 stages with the experts split over 8 GPUs.
  • On the 27B, Megatron-Bridge runs rdsp's exact cut and is still 1.28× slower. We have not profiled where Bridge spends that extra time.

Where rdsp loses

NVIDIA's published fine-tuning setup for these models uses MedPix (medical images with short captions), 32 rows of 4,096 tokens per step. On that setup Megatron-Bridge wins both models.

What changes between receipts and MedPix

  • Lighter images. MedPix rows carry 250 image tokens against 801 for receipts. Bridge's first stage is no longer overloaded, and its tokens/s per GPU rise from 1,890 to 3,238. rdsp's move from 2,567 to 2,341.
  • Fewer, longer rows. 32 rows give rdsp's 8-stage pipeline 16 microbatches per step instead of 64, so GPUs idle longer while the pipeline fills and drains. Bridge's 2-stage pipeline barely notices.
  • Recompute. At 4,096 tokens per row, rdsp's 35B ran out of memory without recompute, so it recomputes every layer. Bridge does not need to.
  • rdsp's image encoder is slow per image. Even with MedPix's small images, its first stage spent about 2.6 s of each 35B step in the encoder (4.16 s against about 1.5 s for the other stages at an even cut).

The first two changed at the same time. One control run would separate them: receipts in MedPix's exact shape (32 × 4,096). It has not been run.

Other places rdsp does not win

  • Megatron-MIMO's 27B layout (vision 1 GPU + TP4 × PP2): MIMO is 1.08× faster. rdsp's two TP4 language stages take about 30 s each.
  • Without a pipeline: on the 27B, TorchTitan's full sharding is 1.37× faster than DeepSpeed ZeRO-3, and Bridge's TP4 × DP2 is 1.09× faster than rdsp on that same layout.
  • Dense 27B on 2 nodes: rdsp's separate vision stage was 16% slower than 8 equal stages (12.06 s against 10.19 s), because the dense language stages became the slowest.

Is 128 × 2,048 a fair setup?

  • It is not NVIDIA's shape. Megatron-Bridge's own Qwen3.5-VL recipes (27B and 35B) all use 32 rows × 4,096 tokens. MIMO's example defaults to 4,096 tokens.
  • 2,048 fits the data. Receipt rows average 1,016 tokens and none is longer than 2,048. At 4,096, three quarters of every row would be padding.
  • The batch was not tuned for rdsp. 64 rows per node came from the first rdsp vs MIMO comparison (Oct 5) and was kept for every run since.
  • It does favor rdsp. More rows per step give the pipeline more microbatches, and receipts are image-heavy. The MedPix table above is the other side, and the slides should say so in a footnote.

How every run was made equal

  • Same rows in the same order: exported once from Megatron-Bridge's data loader and read by every system.
  • Every row padded to the full length (2,048 or 4,096), as Megatron-Bridge does whenever it pipelines or splits experts.
  • Loss averaged over every supervised token (per microbatch in the MIMO comparisons, as MIMO does). AdamW at a constant 1e-5, betas 0.9 / 0.95.
  • bf16 gradients and a 2-byte master copy on rdsp and Megatron-Bridge; TorchTitan reduces gradients in bf16.
  • Losses match across systems. MedPix 35B, steps 1 → 50: Bridge 2.245 → 1.122, rdsp 2.248 → 1.128.

Still open

  1. MedPix footnote on slides 14 and 15. Draft: "On NVIDIA's recipe shape (MedPix, 32 × 4,096, light images) Megatron-Bridge is 1.38× faster; rdsp's lead needs image-heavy rows and enough rows per step."
  2. Slide 7 still says "13–28% faster than Megatron", from the older 2B/4B runs on PCIe GPUs.
  3. Control run: receipts at 32 × 4,096 on 2 nodes, about 45 minutes. It shows whether image share or batch shape flips the MedPix result.
  4. rdsp bug: after a stage runs out of memory, rdsp hangs until the run's timeout instead of exiting (40 minutes lost on Oct 19).
  5. rdsp tensor parallelism leaves Qwen3.5's linear-attention projections unsplit, a likely reason its TP4 stages are slow (not profiled).

Did not run: Megatron-MIMO on its 17-GPU tutorial layout (it needs the same GPU count on every node); rdsp on 17 GPUs (failed at startup with the 1-GPU box); TorchTitan on the 35B MedPix setup (crashed at step 7).
